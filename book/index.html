Why build a book here

RNA Pathology already has the pieces of a learning system. The book will turn those pieces into a more continuous reading path for real-world pathology use.

Reason 1

Pathology-first framing

The goal is not to explain RNA technologies in the abstract, but to connect them to tissue architecture, morphologic context, and practical interpretation choices.

Reason 2

From scattered concepts to sequence

Today the site already covers datasets, challenge questions, and AI workflows. A book will sequence those ideas from fundamentals to advanced case reasoning.

Reason 3

Less ambiguity, more decision support

The strongest chapters will focus on what a result supports, what it does not support, and where pathologists should slow down before making a claim.

Planned chapter map

This is the current draft structure for the online book. The exact chapter order may change as the dataset library and teaching material grow.

Chapter 1

What RNA-based pathology adds

A practical orientation to RNA signals in tissue, why molecular readouts need context, and how RNA-based workflows complement routine pathology rather than replace it.

Chapter 2

Assay selection and question design

When to think in terms of targeted in situ assays, spatial transcriptomics, or broader RNA workflows, and how to match method choice to the actual pathology question.

Chapter 3

Reading signals in tissue context

How morphology, compartment boundaries, gradients, and neighborhood effects should influence interpretation of RNA findings.

Chapter 4

QC checkpoints and common pitfalls

What to watch for before over-calling a signal: technical noise, sampling bias, annotation shortcuts, over-interpretation, and false certainty.

Chapter 5

Organ-based examples

Early examples will likely build on the site's current lung and breast material, expanding from reference galleries toward richer comparative case teaching.

Chapter 6

AI-assisted spatial pathology workflows

A forward-looking section on annotation support, structure discovery, report generation, and where human pathology review remains essential.

Current draft themes

Based on the site as it exists today, these are the themes most ready to become book chapters or short teaching modules.

  • How to think about RNA signals as pathology evidence rather than isolated molecular output
  • Assay selection: targeted in situ methods, spatial transcriptomics, and broader RNA workflows
  • Normal reference patterns versus disease-associated shifts in tissue organization
  • Lung and breast as early organ-based examples for structured case teaching
  • Interpretation boundaries: what a result can suggest versus what it cannot prove alone
  • QC checkpoints before reporting or escalating a finding
  • Pathologist-facing summaries, decision aids, and review-friendly output structure
  • How AI-assisted annotation and structure discovery can support, but not replace, pathology review

Who the book is for

The first edition is likely to stay tightly scoped.

  • Pathologists moving into RNA-based and spatially resolved interpretation
  • Trainees who need a clearer bridge between morphology and molecular readouts
  • Translational teams who want a shared language across pathology, wet lab, and computation
  • Groups using RNA data in tissue settings and wanting a more pathology-grade framing
